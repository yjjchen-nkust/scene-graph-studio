import { Formal, Implications, Intuition, Worked } from './math/Parts';

<Step id="s1">

## A protocol is what the model is handed, not how it is scored

The three protocol names describe three different exams over the same material. PredCls gives the
model the ground-truth boxes and the ground-truth object labels, and asks only for the predicates.
SGCls gives the boxes and withholds the labels. SGDet gives nothing.

The scoring code does not change between them. This is worth saying early, because the names sound
like three metrics and they are not: they are three levels of assistance, and the same matching
rule runs on whatever the model returns. A system that branched on the protocol inside the scorer
would be measuring a fourth thing that nobody publishes against.

</Step>

<Step id="s2">

<Intuition>

Before any number exists, something must decide when a predicted triplet counts as the same
triplet as an annotated one. The definition has two independent halves — did it name the right
things, and did it put them in the right place — and almost every confusing result in this field
comes from those two halves being reported as one.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
\hat t\simeq t\iff\underbrace{c_{\hat s}=c_s\wedge c_{\hat o}=c_o\wedge\hat p=p}_{\text{classification}}\wedge\underbrace{\operatorname{IoU}(b_{\hat s},b_s)\ge\tau\wedge\operatorname{IoU}(b_{\hat o},b_o)\ge\tau}_{\text{localization}}
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat t\simeq t &\iff \Phi_{\text{cls}}\wedge\Phi_{\text{loc}}\\
\Phi_{\text{cls}} &= (c_{\hat s}=c_s)\wedge(c_{\hat o}=c_o)\wedge(\hat p=p)\\
\Phi_{\text{loc}} &= (\operatorname{IoU}_s\ge\tau)\wedge(\operatorname{IoU}_o\ge\tau)
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\neg\Phi_{\text{cls}}\wedge\Phi_{\text{loc}} \equiv \text{right place, wrong name} \quad
\neg\Phi_{\text{loc}}\wedge\Phi_{\text{cls}} \equiv \text{right name, wrong place}
\\[4pt]
\begin{array}{c|cc} & \Phi_{\text{loc}} & \neg\Phi_{\text{loc}}\\ \hline \Phi_{\text{cls}} & \texttt{match} & \texttt{localization}\\ \neg\Phi_{\text{cls}} & \texttt{spurious} & \texttt{spurious}\end{array}\end{gathered}
$$

The four-colour diff you have been reading since L1 is this table on the prediction side, and one
colour more on the ground truth's: `missed` marks an annotated triplet that no prediction matched.
`localization` is the case $\neg\Phi_{\text{loc}}\wedge\Phi_{\text{cls}}$, and it exists so that a
right predicate on a badly placed box does not look the same as a wrong predicate. A wrong name is
`spurious` wherever its box sits: the diff does not separate a wrong name in the right place from
both halves wrong.

</Implications>

</Step>

<Step id="s3">

<Intuition>

The three protocols differ in how much they hand the model, and that difference is a fact about
sets before it is a fact about any number: whatever PredCls lets a model output, SGCls lets it
output too, and SGDet more again. The recall ordering that published tables show is a regularity
observed across models, not a consequence of that inclusion.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
\mathcal{H}_{\text{PredCls}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\text{SGCls}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\text{SGDet}}
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lvert\mathcal{H}_{\text{PredCls}}\rvert &= \lvert V\rvert(\lvert V\rvert-1)\lvert\mathcal{P}\rvert\\
\lvert\mathcal{H}_{\text{SGCls}}\rvert &= \lvert V\rvert(\lvert V\rvert-1)\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert^2\lvert\mathcal{P}\rvert\\
\lvert\mathcal{H}_{\text{SGDet}}\rvert &= B(B-1)\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert^2\lvert\mathcal{P}\rvert
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}R_{\text{SGDet}}@k\le R_{\text{SGCls}}@k\le R_{\text{PredCls}}@k\ \text{is observed, not implied:}
\\ \text{the inclusion shrinks the space a model searches and bounds no model's recall.}
\\[4pt]
\textbf{Note } \lvert V\rvert(\lvert V\rvert-1) \text{ survives under PredCls and SGCls: boxes are given, pairs never are.}\end{gathered}
$$

`Scene-Graph-Benchmark.pytorch` reports the ordering for all four of its reimplemented models at
every K. MOTIFS, for example, reaches R@50 of 32.78 under SGGen, that repository's name for SGDet,
38.92 under SGCls and 65.18 under PredCls (`METRICS.md`, "Recall@K"). Across those rows the
ordering holds without exception, and it remains a measurement of those models: a model that did
worse when handed the true labels would contradict no definition.

The note at the end is the part students skip and professors care about. PredCls hands over the
boxes; it never hands over which pairs are related. The $\lvert V\rvert(\lvert V\rvert-1)$ factor
is present under PredCls and SGCls, which is why the warning `gt_boxes_not_pairs` is attached to
every PredCls and SGCls response in this application without exception.

A PredCls number is therefore not "the easy case". It is the case where the relation reasoning is
measured alone, which is a different claim and a more useful one.

</Implications>

</Step>

<Step id="s4">

## Greedy assignment is an assumption, not a theorem

Matching a ranked list of predictions against the annotation is a bipartite assignment problem. The
field's implementations walk the ranking in score order and take the first available match, which
is greedy. Greedy does not in general attain the maximum matching.

It is nonetheless what the published numbers were computed with, so this project computes it the
same way — and says so. The assumption is discharged on adversarial fixtures rather than asserted:
`data/golden/vectors.json` carries cases built to separate greedy from maximum, and the engine is
held to the greedy answer because that is the answer the literature reports.

Notice which kind of claim this is. "Greedy equals maximum here" is empirical and checkable.
"Greedy is what everyone computes" is a fact about the field. Neither is a theorem, and the
distinction is why the engine's documentation calls it an assumption.

</Step>

<Step id="s5">

## A missing annotation counts as a mistake

The matching rule has no category for "true but unrecorded". A predicted triplet that holds of the
photograph and is absent from the annotation is scored `spurious`, identically to one that is
simply wrong.

This is not an implementation choice that could be fixed. It follows from the annotation being
incomplete (M1) and the metric having only the annotation to compare against. Its consequences run
through the rest of the course: it puts a ceiling on measurable precision, it rewards models that
predict the head of the distribution, and it makes the `spurious` colour in the diff the one a
student should always look at twice.

</Step>
