import { Formal, Implications, Intuition, Worked } from './math/Parts';

<Step id="s1">

## The sentence this module is built around

A frontier vision-language model asked plainly for scene-graph triplets scores 0.032 on R@20 —
not because it cannot see, but because it does not know which words are allowed.

Everything that follows is an elaboration of that sentence. The paper's contribution is not
architectural. It is a claim that constraining a VLM's output vocabulary, plus a self-audit,
substitutes for a trained relation head, and every figure in its evaluation should be read against
that claim and no other.

You are eleven modules in for a reason. Without M4 you cannot see why 0.032 is a statement about
string matching rather than evidence of a blind model; without M6 you cannot see why the mean
recall gains matter more than the recall gains.

</Step>

<Step id="s2">

## Why the prior art fails here — the paper's own argument

§2.2 makes four specific complaints. They are four, not general dissatisfaction.

**Boxes are loose.** A bounding box covers pixels belonging to other objects, and in overlapping
regions the assignment is arbitrary. You met this bound in M2.

**Boxes miss unbounded regions.** The paper's own example is a road area: critical to the scene and
not a boxable object.

**Box annotation carries junk.** Repeated markings of trees and sky contribute nothing and produce
isolated nodes, and learning from them confuses the model.

**Panoptic SGG fixes those three and creates a new one.** Per-pixel labelling is more faithful and
far more expensive — and, in the authors' words, in some PSG datasets the top three relationship
categories account for over 50 per cent of samples while many rare relations occupy less than
1 per cent.

Dwell on the last. That is the long-tail problem, stated by the authors of the method under study,
and it is exactly what mR@K exists to expose. Having read it here, go back and read M4 again.

</Step>

<Step id="s3">

**A note on notation before the equations.** This paper reuses two letters the course has already
spent, and a third the course is about to spend. Its $E$ is the set of worked examples handed to the model, not the relationship set of a
scene graph (M0). Its $N$ is the number of expert audits, not the count of ground-truth triplets
(M1, M4). Both are quoted here exactly as the paper writes them, because a reader who meets the
equations in the paper will meet these letters. The project's symbol table glosses each letter once,
at its course-wide use, and a local meaning is stated where it occurs — here. Its $q$, in the audit reconstruction below, is the probability that one audit corrects an error; M7 spends the same letter on DETR's query count, which is the standard use.


<Intuition>

Equation (1) is the naive call: hand over the frame and an instruction. Equation (2) hands over the
dictionaries first. The two differ by their arguments and by nothing else — same operator, same
image, same prompt template — so the ablation between them isolates a single variable.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
out_{trpl}=\mathrm{VLM}(V_t,\textit{Prompt}) \qquad (1)
$$

$$
out^{s_1}_t=\mathrm{VLM}(V_t,\,O,\,P,\,E,\,\textit{Prompt}) \qquad (2)
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)\ \ out_{trpl} &= \mathrm{VLM}(V_t,\textit{Prompt}) && R@20 = 0.032\\
(2)\ \ out^{s_1}_t &= \mathrm{VLM}(V_t,O,\textit{Prompt}) && R@20 = 1.787\\
(2)\ \ out^{s_1}_t &= \mathrm{VLM}(V_t,P,\textit{Prompt}) && R@20 = 2.079\\
(2)\ \ out^{s_1}_t &= \mathrm{VLM}(V_t,O,P,\textit{Prompt}) && R@20 = 20.792\\
(2)\ \ out^{s_1}_t &= \mathrm{VLM}(V_t,O,P,E,\textit{Prompt}) && R@20 = 23.040
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\text{Same operator, same image, same prompt template. The only varying argument is } (O,P,E).
\\ \text{Rows 2 and 3 are single-component ablations, not cumulative: } O \text{ alone, then } P \text{ alone.}
\\ \text{The jump is superadditive: } 1.787 \text{ and } 2.079 \text{ apart, } 20.792 \text{ together.}
\\ \text{Ratio between endpoints: } 23.040/0.032 = 720. \ \text{Architectural difference: none.}\end{gathered}
$$

The superadditivity is the most interesting quantitative fact in the paper and the text does not
remark on it. Constraining one end of the triplet is nearly worthless; constraining both is
transformative. Read Table 3 as the five-row factorial it is — an earlier summary of this paper
presented it as a four-row cumulative sequence, which hides the $P$-alone row and with it the whole
observation.

The paper's own reading of rows 2 and 3 is that $P$ alone beats $O$ alone "because predicting
relations is often more challenging than forecasting object categories". Defensible, and it rests
on a 0.292-point difference at R@20 on a 10 per cent subset.

</Implications>

</Step>

<Step id="s4">

<Intuition>

Step 1's output contains noise. Equation (3) shows it back to the same model $N$ times
independently, with the frame still in hand, and asks each copy to audit it; equation (4)
reconciles the audits into one graph with their reasoning available to the reconciler.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
\bigl(out^{s_2}_t,a_i\bigr)=\mathrm{VLM}\bigl(V_t,O,P,E,\textit{Prompt},out^{s_1}_t\bigr),\ i\in\{1,\dots,N\} \qquad (3)
$$

$$
out^{s_3}_t=\mathrm{VLM}\bigl(out^{s_2}_t,\alpha,\textit{Prompt}\bigr) \qquad (4)
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{step 2 draws } N \text{ i.i.d. audits } a_1,\dots,a_N \text{ of } out^{s_1}_t; \text{ step 3 aggregates them.}\\
\text{If each audit corrects an error independently with probability } q,\\
\Pr[\text{error survives } N \text{ audits}] = (1-q)^N \quad\text{(exponential in } N)\\
\text{cost} = N+2 \ \text{VLM calls per frame} \quad\text{(linear in } N)
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\text{Measured: } N=1,2,3,5 \Rightarrow R@20 = 21.323,\ 21.584,\ 23.158,\ 23.287.
\\ \text{At } k=20 \text{ that favours } N=5 \text{, but not everywhere:}
\\ mR@50 = 25.480 \text{ at } N=3 \text{ against } 24.383 \text{ at } N=5 \text{; } R@100 = 30.142 \text{ against } 30.020.
\\ \text{So } N=3 \text{ is not dominated, and the recommendation rests on more than token cost.}
\\ \text{An empirical claim with no accompanying theory — the independence assumption is untested.}\end{gathered}
$$

Two warnings, and both are about reading rather than about the method.

**The exponential-versus-linear argument is ours, not the paper's.** The paper offers no theory for
why $N$ audits help. The reconstruction above rests on independence, and three samples from one
model on one frame are correlated, so the assumption is plainly false. Present it to a student as a
hypothesis to break, never as the authors' reasoning.

**A lab that shows only the `@20` column teaches the opposite of what the data says.** At $k=20$,
$N=5$ wins on both axes. Across all six cutoffs it does not. This is why L5 commits all four rows
at all six cutoffs, with a test asserting the two cutoffs where $N=5$ loses.

The shape of the curve — steep from 1 to 3, then flat and inconsistent in sign from 3 to 5 — is
what a variance-reduction mechanism looks like when it saturates. That supports the paper's choice
without supplying the theory it lacks.

</Implications>

</Step>

<Step id="s5">

<Intuition>

Why does 0.032 happen at all? Because a predicate outside the allowed vocabulary cannot match
anything, and the slot it occupied is gone. One mistake, counted twice.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
\hat p\notin\mathcal{P}\ \Longrightarrow\ \hat t\not\simeq t\ \ \forall t\in G\quad(\text{one ranked slot spent, and } t \text{ left unmatched})
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat p\notin\mathcal{P} \;&\Rightarrow\; \hat p\ne p \ \ \forall t\in G &&\text{Definition 5, third conjunct}\\
&\Rightarrow\; \hat t\not\simeq t \ \ \forall t \;\Rightarrow\; \hat t \text{ contributes nothing to } \mu\\
&\Rightarrow\; \hat t \text{ occupies a slot in } X_k \text{ and the true } t \text{ stays unmatched}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\textbf{The error is counted twice:} \text{ once as a wasted rank, once as a miss.}
\\ \text{This is what LLM4SGG names } \textit{semantic over-simplification}, \text{ and why zero-shot}
\\ \text{frontier VLMs score } R@50\approx 1\text{–}2 \text{ on VG-150 while describing the scene correctly.}\end{gathered}
$$

This is the third conjunct of the match relation you met in M3, doing exactly what it was defined
to do. Nothing is broken. A metric that compares strings will punish a synonym, and M1 already told
you the vocabulary is full of synonyms with no hierarchy.

$O$ and $P$ are therefore not hints. They are a projection of free-text output onto a closed
vocabulary, applied *before* generation rather than after — and whether the "before" matters is the
paper's untested assumption. Step 8 returns to it.

</Implications>

</Step>

<Step id="s6">

## What Table 2 actually shows

On VG-150 the method reports R@50 of 30.48 and mR@50 of 21.39; on PSG, 29.69 and 25.12.

**The mean-recall gains dwarf the recall gains, and that is the real evidence.** On VG, mR@50
improves by 9.19 against R@50's 2.58; on PSG, 8.12 against 6.39. A method that lifts mean recall
far harder than recall is recovering *tail* predicates, which is consistent with the claimed
mechanism: a fixed dictionary $P$ gives a rare predicate the same access to the output as a common
one, and a frequency-trained classifier never does. If one thing is taught from Table 2, teach
this.

**GPT-4V beats Gemini-Pro-Vision on ISG at every cutoff**, and the gap widens with $K$ — 3.78
points at R@20 becoming 11.71 at R@100. The paper attributes nothing to this, and neither should
you: it is an uncontrolled comparison of two proprietary models at two moments in time.

**Do not compare across the three datasets.** Different splits, different vocabularies, different
image sources. The three blocks are three separate experiments that happen to share a row label.

</Step>

<Step id="s7">

## ISG, and the industrial gap that produced it

The dataset's argument is density, not scale. ISG holds 10K images against PSG's 49K, but carries
688 object classes to PSG's 133, 144 predicate classes to 56, and 17.6 predicates per image
against 5.7. It is the only row in the paper's Table 1 with a tick in the industrial column.

Its construction is three steps: collection from five sources — CHICO, Ego4D, HA-ViD, IKEA ASM and
CALVIN — with the top ten triplets labelled; automatic extraction of a concise object and predicate
dictionary from noisy VLM-generated graphs; then manual filtering, alignment to $O$ and $P$, and
verification.

**ISG is request-only.** There is no public release and no repository; the data-availability
statement commits only to release on request. This course therefore teaches ISG from the published
tables and figures, labels it request-only, and warns about the unrelated `ISG-Bench` name
collision. In its place the application ships a mini-ISG built by the same method from openly
licensed industrial frames, labelled unambiguously as this project's teaching set and not the
authors'.

</Step>

<Step id="s8">

## What the paper does not establish

State these explicitly. They are where the work is honest, and where it is thin.

**The backbone is uncontrolled, and the paper says so.** Its stated limitation is that the backbone
of different VLMs may influence performance. Both models tested are now superseded and neither is
reproducible at the version tested, so every IndVisSGG figure in Table 2 measures a model that no
longer exists in that form.

**No theory for $N$.** Step 4 above. The independence assumption is untested and almost certainly
false.

**The ablation's baseline may be unfairly weak, and this is the most serious methodological gap.**
Row 1 of Table 3 uses an unconstrained prompt. A stronger naive baseline exists and is not
reported: free-text output followed by nearest-neighbour projection onto $O$ and $P$ *after*
generation. Without it, Table 3 conflates two claims — that constraining the vocabulary helps, and
that constraining it before generation helps — and only the second is the method. A student who
runs that missing experiment in L5 has done something the paper did not.

**Cross-method comparability.** Table 2's baselines are the paper's own re-implementations, and the
paper states neither their detector backbones nor their epoch budgets. It names no evaluation
protocol and no constraint mode anywhere either: the strings SGDet, PredCls and SGCls do not
occur in it, and §5.1.3 says only that “both $O$ and $P$ are predetermined for all methods”. So
the columns cannot be shown to be comparable, which is a stronger and less flattering statement
than saying they differ. That is a field-wide problem rather than this paper's alone, and it is
why this application renders per-paper tables behind a non-comparability banner and never merges
them into one ranking.

</Step>
