<Step id="s1">

## Three layers, annotated by different people

Visual Genome is not one annotation. It is a stack: regions with free-text descriptions, objects
grounded in boxes, attributes on those objects, and relationships between them — each layer
collected separately, by different crowd workers, with no requirement that the layers agree.

Two consequences arrive before any model does. The predicate vocabulary is whatever people wrote,
so `on`, `on top of`, `sitting on` and `resting on` all exist and none of them is a parent of
another. And the relationship layer is radically incomplete: an annotator recorded a few edges
per image out of the hundreds that hold, and nothing marks which of the rest were considered and
rejected.

The course returns to the second point repeatedly, because every metric in the field treats the
annotation as if it were the truth.

</Step>

<Step id="s2">

<Intuition>

Four predicates that mean the same thing are four separate classes to the metric. A model that
answers `on` when the annotator wrote `sitting on` is scored exactly as wrong as one that answers
`eating`. Merging synonyms is therefore not tidying: it changes the number, on the same model,
without retraining anything.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
p_1,p_2\mapsto\tilde p \;\Rightarrow\; \lvert\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\rvert\downarrow,\qquad mR@k=\frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\rvert}\sum_{p}R_p@k\ \uparrow
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{merge } p_1,p_2\mapsto\tilde p:\quad n_{\tilde p}&=n_{p_1}+n_{p_2}, &&C\mapsto C-1\\
R_{\tilde p}@k &= \frac{\mu(G^{(p_1)}\cup G^{(p_2)},X_k)}{n_{p_1}+n_{p_2}} \;\ge\; \frac{\max_i \mu(G^{(p_i)},X_k)}{n_{p_1}+n_{p_2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\text{A prediction of } p_1 \text{ against ground truth } p_2 \text{ spent a ranked slot } \textit{and} \text{ left the truth unmatched.}
\\ \text{After merging it is a match, so } mR \text{ rises with the model unchanged.}\end{gathered}
$$

Two papers that merge differently report numbers that cannot be compared, and neither is wrong.
This is the first of several reasons a table of published figures is not a ranking.

</Implications>

</Step>

<Step id="s3">

<Intuition>

The predicate distribution is not merely uneven, it is Zipf-like: a handful of spatial predicates
cover most of the annotation and the rest are rare. A model that learns only the head can post a
high recall while knowing nothing about any relation that carries information. Mean recall exists
to make that visible, and the arithmetic below says exactly when the two metrics agree.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
n_p\propto p^{-s},\qquad R_p=\Bigl(\tfrac{n_p}{n_1}\Bigr)^{\gamma},\qquad R=\sum_p\frac{n_p}{N}R_p,\qquad mR=\frac{1}{C}\sum_p R_p
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_p &\propto p^{-s},\quad p=1,\dots,C &&\text{Zipf}\\
R_p &= (n_p/n_1)^{\gamma}=p^{-s\gamma} &&\text{head-biased model}\\
R &= \sum_p \frac{n_p}{N}R_p = \frac{\sum_p p^{-s(1+\gamma)}}{\sum_p p^{-s}} = \frac{H_C^{(s+s\gamma)}}{H_C^{(s)}} &&\text{generalized harmonic numbers}\\
mR &= \frac{1}{C}\sum_p p^{-s\gamma} = \frac{H_C^{(s\gamma)}}{C}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}s=0 \;\Rightarrow\; n_p \text{ constant} \;\Rightarrow\; \operatorname{Cov}(n,R)=0 \;\Rightarrow\; R=mR \ \text{ exactly.}\end{gathered}
$$

Read the condition in the other direction: $R$ and $mR$ separate exactly to the extent that a
model's per-predicate recall is correlated with predicate frequency. The gap between them is not
a second measurement of quality. It is a measurement of that correlation.

</Implications>

</Step>

<Step id="s4">

## `VG150` names three different datasets

$$
\texttt{VG150}\ \mapsto\ \text{three distinct }(\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}},\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}},\mathcal{D}_{\text{test}})\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{numbers not comparable}
$$

The 150-object, 50-predicate subset of Visual Genome has been redistributed in several splits
that share a name and not a test set. A figure from one split placed in a table beside a figure
from another is not a comparison, and nothing in either paper's notation says which was used.

This project therefore never writes bare `vg150`. The dataset identifier is `vg150-sgb`, naming
the redistribution, and decision D-09 forbids the short form anywhere in the codebase — a rule
enforced by lint rather than by memory, because the two names are one character apart.

</Step>

<Step id="s5">

## In the lab

L1 gives you an image whose objects are already boxed and asks for the edges. Build a few
triplets, submit, and look at which of the annotated edges you missed. Then ask the harder
question: of the edges you proposed that came back `spurious`, how many are actually true of the
photograph and simply absent from the annotation?

</Step>

## Checkpoint

Suppose a paper merges `on` and `sitting on` and reports mR@50 two points above the previous
best. State what you would need from the paper to decide whether the model improved. Then state
what you would need to decide whether the *field* improved.
