import { Formal, Implications, Intuition, Worked } from './math/Parts';

<Step id="s1">

## Detect, enumerate, classify

For five years the field had one architecture. Run a detector; take every ordered pair of
detections; classify a relation on each pair. Everything between 2017 and 2021 is a variation on
what happens in the third stage, and the interesting failures are all consequences of the second.

</Step>

<Step id="s2">

<Intuition>

Enumerating pairs is quadratic in the number of detections, and the relation classifier must
decide on every one. The number of true relations is linear. That ratio is the whole economics of
the two-stage design, and it is worse than it sounds.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
\text{cost}=N(N-1)\lvert\mathcal{P}\rvert=O\!\left(N^{2}\lvert\mathcal{P}\rvert\right),\qquad \text{true relations}=O(N)
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{pairs} &= N(N-1) &&\text{ordered, } i\ne j\\
\text{decisions} &= N(N-1)\lvert\mathcal{P}\rvert = O\bigl(N^2\lvert\mathcal{P}\rvert\bigr)\\
\lvert E\rvert &= O(N) &&\text{true relations, empirically}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}N=80,\ \lvert\mathcal{P}\rvert=310:\quad 80\cdot 79\cdot 310 = 1{,}958{,}800 \ \text{decisions for} \approx 20 \ \text{relations}
\\[4pt]
\text{positive rate} \approx 1.0\times 10^{-5}. \text{ This ratio is the motivation for one-stage set prediction.}\end{gathered}
$$

One decision in a hundred thousand is positive. A classifier trained on that distribution learns
to say "no relation" and is right almost always, which is why every two-stage method spends its
capacity on pair *pruning* and on priors — and why the frequency baseline of M4 does so well.

This ratio is also the motivation for the one-stage set prediction of M7. If the pairs are the
problem, stop enumerating them.

</Implications>

</Step>

<Step id="s3">

<Intuition>

IMP's idea is that a relation's evidence lives partly in its neighbours, so let nodes exchange
beliefs and iterate. The idea is right and the arithmetic has a trap: run the same averaging long
enough and every node holds the same belief.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
b^{(t+1)}_i=(1-w)\,b^{(0)}_i+w\,\overline{b^{(t)}_{\mathcal{N}(i)}}\ \xrightarrow[\ t\to\infty\ ]{w>0}\ \overline{b^{(0)}}\quad(\text{consensus; information destroyed})
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
b^{(t+1)}_i &= (1-w)\,b^{(0)}_i + w\,\overline{b^{(t)}} , \qquad \overline{b^{(t)}}=\tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_j b^{(t)}_j\\
\overline{b^{(t+1)}} &= (1-w)\overline{b^{(0)}} + w\,\overline{b^{(t)}} \;\Rightarrow\; \overline{b^{(t)}}=\overline{b^{(0)}} \ \forall t
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\text{So } b^{(t)}_i = (1-w^t)\bigl[(1-w)b^{(0)}_i + w\overline{b^{(0)}}\bigr]\cdot\!\ldots\ \longrightarrow\
b^{\ast}_i = (1-w)b^{(0)}_i + w\,\overline{b^{(0)}}
\\[4pt]
\text{Spread contracts by } (1-w) \text{ per step: } \ \operatorname{Var}(b^{(t)}) = (1-w)^{2}\operatorname{Var}(b^{(0)}) \text{ at the fixed point.}
\\ w\to 1 \Rightarrow \text{every belief equals the graph mean and the model distinguishes nothing.}\end{gathered}
$$

The fixed point is a contraction, not a consensus that preserves anything. At $w \to 1$ every
belief equals the graph mean and the model distinguishes nothing at all — it has averaged away the
signal it was passing.

Everything after IMP is an answer to this. Neural Motifs replaces free message passing with an
LSTM over a fixed object ordering plus an explicit frequency bias. VCTree learns a tree rather than
a complete graph, so the averaging is structured. GPS-Net adds direction awareness and a node
priority so that not every neighbour counts equally. KERN and BGNN route by class and resample the
tail.

Read them as four attempts to stop the contraction without losing the context.

</Implications>

</Step>

<Step id="s4">

## What each one actually contributed

**Neural Motifs (2018)** observed that object labels alone predict relations remarkably well, and
built that in as an explicit frequency bias over a context LSTM. Its own FREQ baseline is the one
that embarrassed the field, and it is published inside this paper.

**VCTree (2019)** replaces the fixed ordering with a learned dynamic tree, so context flows along a
structure chosen per image rather than a chain chosen by the implementer.

**GPS-Net (2020)** adds three things the averaging above destroys: direction-aware message passing,
a node-priority loss so that a confident object is not overwritten by an uncertain neighbour, and
an adaptive reasoning module.

**KERN and BGNN** attack the tail directly, routing messages by class and resampling the
distribution. They are the bridge to M6, where debiasing becomes the whole agenda.

Mean recall arrived in 2019, introduced independently by KERN and by VCTree. Everything before it
was ranked on a metric that could not see the tail.

</Step>

<Step id="s5">

## In the lab

L4 runs the committed predictions of these models on one image side by side. Look first at where
they agree: the head predicates, which the frequency prior alone would have got. Then look at the
tail, and at how little separates four years of architecture.

</Step>
