<Step id="s1">

## Detect, enumerate, classify

For five years the field had one architecture. Run a detector; take every ordered pair of
detections; classify a relation on each pair. Everything between 2017 and 2021 is a variation on
what happens in the third stage, and the interesting failures are all consequences of the second.

</Step>

<Step id="s2">

<Intuition>

Enumerating pairs is quadratic in the number of detections, and the relation classifier must
decide on every one. The number of true relations is linear. That ratio is the whole economics of
the two-stage design, and it is worse than it sounds.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
\text{cost}=N(N-1)\lvert\mathcal{P}\rvert=O\!\left(N^{2}\lvert\mathcal{P}\rvert\right),\qquad \text{true relations}=O(N)
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{pairs} &= N(N-1) &&\text{ordered, } i\ne j\\
\text{decisions} &= N(N-1)\lvert\mathcal{P}\rvert = O\bigl(N^2\lvert\mathcal{P}\rvert\bigr)\\
\lvert E\rvert &= O(N) &&\text{true relations, empirically}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\lvert\mathcal{P}\rvert=50\ (\text{VG150}),\ N=12:\quad 12\cdot 11=132 \ \text{pairs},\quad 132\cdot 50=6{,}600 \ \text{decisions for} \approx 7 \ \text{relations}
\\ N=80 \ \text{boxes kept}:\quad 80\cdot 79=6{,}320 \ \text{pairs},\quad 6{,}320\cdot 50=316{,}000 \ \text{decisions for the same} \approx 7
\\[4pt]
\text{positive rate} \approx 1.1\times 10^{-3} \ \text{at } N=12, \quad \approx 2.2\times 10^{-5} \ \text{at } N=80
\\ \text{The 80 VG150 frames bundled here: } 651 \text{ of } 26{,}282 \text{ ordered pairs carry a relation.}\end{gathered}
$$

VG150's dataset card gives its validation split 5,000 images, 62,754 object annotations and 33,203
relations: 12.6 objects and 6.6 relations per image. At twelve objects about one decision in a
thousand is positive, and about one in forty-five thousand once a detector keeps 80 boxes, a
setting chosen here rather than a figure from a paper. Across the 80 VG150 frames this course
bundles, 651 of 26,282 ordered pairs carry any relation, 2.5 per cent, before the 50 predicates
multiply the decisions. A classifier trained on that distribution learns to say "no relation" and
is right almost always, which is why every two-stage method spends its capacity on pair *pruning*
and on priors, and why the frequency baseline of M4 does so well.

This ratio is also the motivation for the one-stage set prediction of M7. If the pairs are the
problem, stop enumerating them.

</Implications>

</Step>

<Step id="s4">

<Intuition>

IMP's idea is that a relation's evidence lives partly in its neighbours, so let nodes exchange
beliefs and iterate. The idea is right, and the arithmetic has a trap: averaging alone, repeated,
drives every node to one belief. Keeping a share of each node's own evidence in every round is what
stops it.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
\begin{gathered}
b^{(t+1)}=(1-w)\,b^{(0)}+w\,S\,b^{(t)},\qquad S_{ij}=\tfrac{1}{d_i}\ \text{for } j\in\mathcal{N}(i),\ \text{else } 0,\qquad d_i=\lvert\mathcal{N}(i)\rvert
\\ 0\le w<1:\quad b^{(t)}\longrightarrow b^{\ast}=(1-w)(I-wS)^{-1}\,b^{(0)}
\\ w=1:\quad b^{(t)}_i\longrightarrow\frac{\sum_j d_j\,b^{(0)}_j}{\sum_j d_j}\quad(\text{connected, with an odd cycle})
\end{gathered}
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
b^{\ast} &= (1-w)\,b^{(0)}+w\,S\,b^{\ast} \iff (I-wS)\,b^{\ast}=(1-w)\,b^{(0)} &&\text{the fixed point}\\
\lVert wS\rVert_\infty &= w\,\max_i\textstyle\sum_j S_{ij}\le w<1 \;\Rightarrow\; b^{\ast}=(1-w)\textstyle\sum_{k\ge 0}w^{k}S^{k}\,b^{(0)} &&\text{a mixture of } k\text{-hop averages, every } d_i\ge 1\\
b^{(t+1)}-b^{\ast} &= w\,S\,\bigl(b^{(t)}-b^{\ast}\bigr) \;\Rightarrow\; \lVert b^{(t)}-b^{\ast}\rVert_\infty\le w^{t}\,\lVert b^{(0)}-b^{\ast}\rVert_\infty &&\text{the contraction bound}\\
w=1:\ \ d^{\top}S &= d^{\top} \;\Rightarrow\; d^{\top}b^{(t)}=d^{\top}b^{(0)}\ \text{for every } t &&j\in\mathcal{N}(i)\iff i\in\mathcal{N}(j)\\
b^{(t)}\to c\,\mathbf{1} &\;\Rightarrow\; c=\frac{\sum_j d_j\,b^{(0)}_j}{\sum_j d_j} &&\text{the degree-weighted mean}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\text{Averaging over all } n \text{ nodes, } i \text{ included}:\quad b\mapsto(1-w)\,b^{(0)}+w\,\overline{b}\,\mathbf{1},\ \ \overline{b^{(t)}}=\overline{b^{(0)}}
\\ \Rightarrow\ b^{(1)}=b^{\ast}=(1-w)\,b^{(0)}+w\,\overline{b^{(0)}}\,\mathbf{1}: \ \text{the fixed point in one round; the spread falls once, by the factor } (1-w).
\\[4pt]
w<1:\ b^{\ast} \text{ keeps a } (1-w) \text{ share of each node's own evidence, so no consensus.}
\\ w=1:\ \text{on a connected graph with an odd cycle, consensus at the degree-weighted mean.}\end{gathered}
$$

Averaging alone is what collapses the beliefs; the $(1-w)$ share of each node's own evidence is
what prevents the collapse. For $w < 1$ the beliefs settle on $b^{\ast}$, a mixture of $k$-hop
averages that is not a consensus, and the largest gap to it shrinks at least by the factor $w$ each
round. Only at $w = 1$, where that share is zero, can every belief reach one value. On a connected
graph with an odd cycle they do, at the degree-weighted mean, which can differ from the plain mean
when nodes have unequal numbers of neighbours; on a bipartite graph, a tree among them, the beliefs
can oscillate instead, and on a disconnected one each component with an odd cycle settles on its own
value.

IMP itself (Xu et al. 2017) does not apply this rule. It updates GRU hidden states over a node
graph and an edge graph, pooling messages with learned weights. The rule here is a model of what
repeated averaging does, and the question it poses stands for every method after IMP: how to pass
context without averaging away the signal.

Everything after IMP is an answer to this. Neural Motifs replaces free message passing with an
LSTM over a fixed object ordering plus an explicit frequency bias. VCTree learns a tree rather than
a complete graph, so the averaging is structured. GPS-Net adds direction awareness and a node
priority so that not every neighbour counts equally. KERN and BGNN route by class and resample the
tail.

Read them as four attempts to keep the context without averaging away each node's own evidence.

</Implications>

</Step>

## What each one actually contributed

**Neural Motifs (2018)** observed that object labels alone predict relations remarkably well, and
built that in as an explicit frequency bias over a context LSTM. Its own FREQ baseline is the one
that embarrassed the field, and it is published inside this paper.

**VCTree (2019)** replaces the fixed ordering with a learned dynamic tree, so context flows along a
structure chosen per image rather than a chain chosen by the implementer.

**GPS-Net (2020)** adds three things the averaging above destroys: direction-aware message passing,
a node-priority loss so that a confident object is not overwritten by an uncertain neighbour, and
an adaptive reasoning module.

**KERN and BGNN** attack the tail directly, routing messages by class and resampling the
distribution. They are the bridge to M6, where debiasing becomes the whole agenda.

Mean recall arrived in 2019, introduced independently by KERN and by VCTree. Everything before it
was ranked on a metric that could not see the tail.

</Step>
