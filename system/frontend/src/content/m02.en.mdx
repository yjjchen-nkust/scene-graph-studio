<Step id="s1">

## What "the same object" means to a metric

A model proposes a region and the annotation holds another. Before anything can be called a match
or a mistake, something has to decide whether those two regions are the same object. That decision
is not part of the definition of a scene graph. It is a convention, it has a free parameter, and
the parameter is almost never stated.

</Step>

<Step id="s2">

<Intuition>

Intersection over union asks what fraction of the combined area the two regions agree on. It is
scale-free, which is why it is used, and it is brutal about size: two boxes centred on the same
object but differing enough in scale cannot reach the threshold however well they are placed. The
bound below says exactly how much difference is enough.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
\operatorname{IoU}(b,b^{\prime})=\frac{\lvert b\cap b^{\prime}\rvert}{\lvert b\cup b^{\prime}\rvert},\qquad \text{accept}\iff\operatorname{IoU}\ge\tau
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(b\cap b') &\le \min\bigl(A(b),A(b')\bigr) &&\text{the intersection lies inside both}\\
A(b\cup b') &\ge \max\bigl(A(b),A(b')\bigr) &&\text{the union contains both}\\
\operatorname{IoU} &= \frac{A(b\cap b')}{A(b\cup b')} \;\le\; \frac{\min(A(b),A(b'))}{\max(A(b),A(b'))} &&\text{divide the two bounds}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\text{With } A(b')=\lambda^2 A(b):\quad \operatorname{IoU}\le\min(\lambda^2,\lambda^{-2}),
\quad\text{so } \lambda>\sqrt{2}\ \Rightarrow\ \operatorname{IoU}<\tfrac12 \text{ at } \tau=0.5.\end{gathered}
$$

A box 1.42 times too large in each direction is rejected no matter where it sits. The bound holds
before any question of placement, so a model penalised this way is not being told that it found
the wrong thing — it is being told that it drew the boundary at a different, defensible place.

And $\tau = 0.5$ is a convention. `METRICS.md` does not state it; the field uses it; a student who
believes it is part of the definition will misread every published table. L2's τ slider exists to
make that parameter visible rather than assumed.

A note on notation. The $\lambda$ in that bound is local: it is the ratio of the two boxes' linear
scale and nothing else. M4 uses the same letter for the weight mixing a frequency prior with a
visual model. The collision is in the source literature rather than in this course, and the
symbol table therefore glosses $\lambda$ once, at that later and load-bearing use. Where a symbol
is reused, read the module it belongs to.

</Implications>

## What a box cannot express

A bounding box is an axis-aligned rectangle, so for anything that is not roughly rectangular it
claims area the object does not occupy. Two objects that interleave — a person behind a railing, a
cup on a cluttered table — have boxes that overlap heavily while the objects themselves do not
touch. Any relation scored on box overlap inherits that error.

A panoptic mask assigns every pixel to exactly one segment, which removes the ambiguity and adds
two of its own. Masks are far more expensive to annotate, so datasets that have them are smaller.
And mask overlap needs its own pairing rule, because a subject and an object can share a boundary
in ways two rectangles cannot.

That pairing rule is the `single_mpo` / `multi_mpo` distinction, it changes published numbers, and
L6 exists to score one fixed prediction under both.

</Step>

<Step id="s6">

## PSG: 133 classes, 56 predicates, masks

The Panoptic Scene Graph dataset re-annotates COCO images with panoptic segments rather than
boxes: 80 thing classes and 53 stuff classes, indexed as one concatenated list of 133, with 56
predicates. It is the only Tier-1 dataset in this course that carries masks, which is what makes
the L6 comparison possible at all.

Two things about it are worth knowing before reading any PSG table. Its object vocabulary includes
*stuff* — sky, road, wall — which a box-based dataset cannot represent as an object at all, so its
predicate distribution is not comparable with VG150's. And its images are COCO photographs, whose
licence the annotations' MIT licence does not reach: this project commits PSG annotations and
ships no PSG image.

</Step>
