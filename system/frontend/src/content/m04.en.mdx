<Step id="s1">

## Four metrics, because one was not enough

Recall at a rank cutoff is the only metric this field reports. It has no precision partner, and
that absence is structural rather than an oversight: the annotation is incomplete (M3), so a
prediction absent from the ground truth may be wrong or may be true and unrecorded, and no
precision figure computed against such an annotation would mean anything.

What followed is a sequence of repairs. Mean recall arrived because recall could not tell a model
that understands relations from one that memorised the head of the distribution. No-graph recall
arrived because the constraint mode was silently changing published numbers. Zero-shot recall
arrived because a model can score well on triplets it has seen a thousand times. Each repair
exposes something the previous metric hid, and none of them replaces the others.

</Step>

<Step id="s2">

<Intuition>

Recall at $k$ counts how much of the annotation appears in the model's top $k$ guesses. Raising
$k$ cannot lower it, and nothing anywhere charges for a wrong guess. A model that emits its
fiftieth-best guess loses nothing by doing so.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
R@k=\frac{\lvert G\cap X_k\rvert}{\lvert G\rvert},\qquad k^{\prime}\ge k\Rightarrow R@k^{\prime}\ge R@k \quad(\text{monotone; no precision term exists})
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
k'\ge k &\Rightarrow X_k\subseteq X_{k'} &&\text{the ranking is a prefix order}\\
&\Rightarrow \mu(G,X_k)\le\mu(G,X_{k'}) &&\text{a maximum matching cannot shrink}\\
&\Rightarrow R@k\le R@k' &&\text{divide by } N
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\text{Monotone in } k \text{ with no offsetting term: emitting more guesses never costs anything.}
\\ \text{There is no precision anywhere in this literature.}\end{gathered}
$$

So $k$ is not a detail of presentation. R@20 and R@100 are different quantities, and a table that
mixes them is not a table. This is the first of four parameters that must travel with every number
in this application, which is why `MetricReadout` refuses to render a bare `number`.

</Implications>

</Step>

<Step id="s5">

<Intuition>

The graph constraint says a model may put at most one predicate on each ordered pair. Lift it and
every pair's runner-up enters the ranking too. Nothing about the model changed; the rule for
reading its output did. The effect is not small.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
\pi(\langle s,p,o\rangle)=(s,o);\qquad \text{graph constraint}\iff\forall\,t\ne t^{\prime}\in X_k:\ \pi(t)\ne\pi(t^{\prime})
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
X &= \{t\in X^{\mathrm{ng}} : \sigma(t)=\max_{p}\sigma(\langle s,p,o\rangle)\} &&\text{the constraint keeps the arg-max per pair}\\
&\Rightarrow X\subseteq X^{\mathrm{ng}} \Rightarrow \mu(G,X)\le\mu(G,X^{\mathrm{ng}}) &&\text{the pools nest}\\
X_k &= \text{top-}k(X),\qquad X_k^{\mathrm{ng}}=\text{top-}k(X^{\mathrm{ng}}) &&\text{each cut from its own pool}\\
k\ge\lvert X^{\mathrm{ng}}\rvert &\Rightarrow R@k\le \mathrm{ngR}@k &&\text{at a smaller } k \text{ either side can be larger}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\text{Over the whole pool the gap is strict whenever a ground-truth predicate is some pair's runner-up.}
\\ \text{At } k=2\text{: } (1,2,\text{on},.9),\ (1,2,\text{near},.8),\ (3,4,\text{on},.7) \text{ against } (3,4,\text{on}) \text{ give } R@2=1,\ \mathrm{ngR}@2=0.
\\ \text{STTran on Action Genome: PredCls } R@50 = 71.8 \text{ constrained}, \; 99.1 \text{ unconstrained.}\end{gathered}
$$

Twenty-seven points, from a switch. Papers routinely omit which mode they used, so two figures in
the same table may not be measuring the same thing. Claim `c-m04-sttran-constraint` in this
module's frontmatter carries that pair of numbers marked `verified: false`: the source note this
course inherited does not name the table, and until someone opens the paper the figure is a
reported quantity rather than a checked one.

</Implications>

## The top k of each pool

<Playground kp="E4" part="2" />

<Step id="s8">

<Intuition>

Mean recall weights every predicate equally instead of weighting by how often it occurs. The two
metrics are the same average under different weights, and the difference between them turns out to
be a single covariance. That identity is the whole bias problem written in one line.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
w_p\propto n_p^{\alpha};\qquad \underbrace{R@k=\sum_p\frac{n_p}{\lvert G\rvert}R_p@k}_{\alpha=1}\qquad \underbrace{mR@k=\sum_p\frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\rvert}R_p@k}_{\alpha=0}
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
R@k-mR@k &= \sum_p \frac{n_p}{N}R_p - \frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\rvert}\sum_p R_p &&\text{Definition 9, } p\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\\
&= \frac{1}{N}\sum_p\Bigl(n_p-\frac{N}{\lvert\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\rvert}\Bigr)R_p = \frac{1}{N}\sum_p (n_p-\bar n)R_p &&\bar n=N/\lvert\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\rvert\\
&= \frac{1}{N}\sum_p (n_p-\bar n)(R_p-\bar R) &&\textstyle\sum_p(n_p-\bar n)=0\\
&= \frac{\lvert\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\rvert}{N}\operatorname{Cov}(n,R) = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(n,R)}{\bar n}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\text{As } \alpha \text{ moves from 1 to 0 in } w_p(\alpha)\propto n_p^{\alpha}, \text{ the weighted mean moves from } R \text{ to } mR.
\\ \text{The whole bias problem is the sign of one covariance.}\end{gathered}
$$

Read the third line carefully: the step that introduces $\bar R$ is legitimate because
$\sum_p(n_p-\bar n)=0$, so subtracting a constant from $R_p$ changes nothing. What remains is
exactly $\operatorname{Cov}(n,R)$ divided by the mean predicate frequency.

The consequence is worth stating plainly. $R > mR$ says the model is better at frequent predicates
than at rare ones. It does not say the model is good, and it does not say it is bad. It is a
measurement of correlation, and M6 is the story of what happens when a field tries to drive a
correlation to zero.

</Implications>

</Step>

<Step id="s9">

<Intuition>

No-graph recall is the constraint of step 5 relaxed by degrees rather than switched off: allow
$m$ predicates per pair instead of one. Increasing $m$ can only add candidates to the pool, which
stops growing once $m$ passes the number of predicates the model scores for any pair. Recall at a
fixed $k$ need not rise with it: every runner-up admitted takes a place in the top $k$ from
something else.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
\mathrm{ngR}@k:\ \pi \text{ need not be injective};\qquad \lvert\{t\in X_k:\pi(t)=(s,o)\}\rvert\le n_{\text{pair}}
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
X^{\mathrm{ng}}(m) &= \bigcup_{(s,o)}\bigl\{\text{top-}m\text{ predicates for }(s,o)\bigr\} &&\text{the pool with } m \text{ per pair}\\
m'\ge m &\Rightarrow X^{\mathrm{ng}}(m)\subseteq X^{\mathrm{ng}}(m') &&\text{the pool grows with } m\\
k\ge\lvert X^{\mathrm{ng}}(m')\rvert &\Rightarrow \mathrm{ngR}_m@k\le \mathrm{ngR}_{m'}@k &&\text{at a smaller } k \text{ it need not}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\text{The pool stops growing once } m \text{ reaches the most predicates any pair carries.}
\\ \text{A model scoring all } \lvert\mathcal{P}\rvert=50 \text{ predicates per pair grows its pool until } m=50.\end{gathered}
$$

The application's third mode, `semi`, sits between the two: at most $m$ predicates
per ordered object pair, with $m = 2$. It is a cap, not the Semi Constraint STTran proposed for Action Genome, which keeps for each
object pair the top attention predicate and every spatial or contacting predicate scoring above
0.9 (M12 gives its source); the application approximates that rule and does not compute it.

</Implications>

</Step>

<Step id="s12">

<Intuition>

Now the result that shaped the field. Consider a predictor that reads no pixels at all: given the
subject and object classes, it emits the most frequent predicate for that class pair. Mix it with
a visual model and watch what each metric does as the mixing weight moves.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
\sigma_p(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)\underbrace{\Pr[p\mid c_s,c_o]}_{\text{no pixels consulted}}+\lambda\underbrace{f_\theta(V,s,o)}_{\text{visual model}}
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_p(\lambda) &= (1-\lambda)\,\pi_p + \lambda\, f_p, \qquad \pi_p=\Pr[p\mid c_s,c_o],\ f_p=f_\theta(V,s,o)\\
R(\lambda) &= R@k \text{ of the ranking by } \sigma(\lambda), \text{ and likewise } mR(\lambda)\\
&\text{constant in } \lambda \text{ except where two blended scores cross and the ranking changes}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\text{The blended score is affine in } \lambda\text{; recall over its ranking is piecewise constant.}
\\ \text{At } \lambda=0 \text{ the predictor reads no pixels, yet}
\\ \operatorname{Cov}(n,\pi)\gg 0 \text{ by construction, so } R \text{ is high and } mR \text{ is near zero.}
\\[4pt]
\text{VG150, PredCls } mR@100: \ \text{FREQ } 16.0 \; > \; \text{MOTIFS } 15.3 \; > \; \text{IMP}^{+}\ 10.5.\end{gathered}
$$

Three things about that last line, in order of how often they are got wrong.

**The baseline was published inside the paper it embarrasses.** Zellers et al. put FREQ in the
Neural Motifs paper itself (CVPR 2018), where on $R@K$ it beat the learned models of its era.

**Mean recall did not exist yet.** It arrived a year later, introduced independently by KERN and by
VCTree, both CVPR 2019, precisely because $R@K$ cannot separate understanding from memorisation.
Attributing mR to the 2018 paper reverses the chronology and the argument.

**Read the row, not the headline.** The figure is IMP*+*, not IMP. And KERN's own Table 1 puts the
same FREQ and MOTIFS at 15.8 and 14.4. Two tables, two answers, the same two methods — which is
why this module's frontmatter carries `source_table` on every claim, and why a mean-recall figure
quoted without its table is not yet a fact.

</Implications>

</Step>

<Step id="s13">

<Intuition>

On a dataset with masks, a further parameter appears that has nothing to do with the model: may
one subject-object pair be counted through several masks, or exactly one? Permitting several costs
a one-stage model nothing and pays it well.

</Intuition>

<Formal>

$$
\text{MultiMPO permits }\lvert\{m:\pi(m)=(s,o)\}\rvert>1 \;\Longrightarrow\; \widehat{mR}=\rho\cdot mR,\quad\rho>1
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{SingleMPO}&: \ \lvert\{m:\pi(m)=(s,o)\}\rvert = 1\\
\text{MultiMPO}&: \ \lvert\{m:\pi(m)=(s,o)\}\rvert = d \ge 1 \ \text{permitted}\\
\widehat{\mu} &= \textstyle\sum_{(s,o)}\min\bigl(d,\ \text{true matches at }(s,o)\bigr) \;\ge\; \mu
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\text{A one-stage model emits duplicate masks freely, so } d>1 \text{ costs it nothing and pays.}
\\ \text{A two-stage model emits one mask per instance, so } d=1 \text{ regardless.}
\\[4pt]
\text{Measured: PSGTR } 20.8\to 11.62,\quad \text{HiLo } 30.3\to 18.33,\quad \text{VCTree } \approx\text{unchanged.}\end{gathered}

nothing-to-lose is not a neutral rule, and the gap it opened ran for years before it was named.

produces rather than reads about.

R@K \text{ on VRD depends on } m=\lvert\{p:\langle s,p,o\rangle\in X\}\rvert\in\{1,10,70\},\ \text{usually undeclared}
$$

</Formal>

<Worked>

$$
\begin{aligned}
&R@K \text{ on VRD admits } m \text{ predicates per ordered pair}, \ m\in\{1,10,70\} &&\text{VRD papers call it } k\\
m=1 &\equiv \text{graph constraint} \qquad m=\lvert\mathcal{P}\rvert=70 \equiv \text{no constraint at all}\\
m=70 &\Rightarrow \text{every predicate of every pair is a candidate; the cut at } K \text{ still ranks them}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Worked>

<Implications>

$$
\begin{gathered}\text{At } m=70 \text{ no predicate is excluded for its pair; only the cut at } K \text{ excludes a candidate.}
\\ \text{Papers routinely omit } m, \text{ so VRD numbers are not comparable across papers by default.}\end{gathered}
$$

At the top of that range the rule that excluded a pair's runner-ups is gone: every predicate of
every pair is a candidate, and only the cut at $K$ decides which reach the score, by comparing
candidates across pairs. A model is therefore still rewarded for scoring the right relation high,
though no longer for ranking it first within its pair. Phrase detection, $PR@K$, is a different
relaxation: it tests one box around the whole pair instead of one box per object. Nothing here
orders the two metrics; as with the protocol ordering of M3, any gap between them is measured, not
derived.

</Implications>

## Four more things the number does not tell you

**Zero-shot recall** restricts the ground truth to triplets whose exact class signature never
appeared in training. It collapses for most models, and the collapse is invisible in $R$ and in
$mR$ alike, because the zero-shot triplets are a small fraction of the total.

**Open Images uses a different metric family** — a weighted combination of relationship detection
mAP, phrase detection mAP and recall — so a figure from that leaderboard is not comparable with a
VG150 figure at all, regardless of protocol and constraint.

**Cross-paper comparison is the default failure.** Split, constraint mode, $\tau$, mask pairing,
predicate merging, $k$: six parameters, each changing the number, each routinely unstated. This
application attaches four of them to every `MetricValue` for that reason, and marks the rest in
the warning list.

**Calibration is absent from the field.** No standard SGG metric asks whether a model's confidence
means anything. A model whose top-ranked guess is right 40 per cent of the time and one whose
top-ranked guess is right 90 per cent of the time can post the same $R@50$.

</Step>
