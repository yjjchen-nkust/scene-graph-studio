<Step id="s1">

## Thing and stuff

Panoptic segmentation divides an image into segments of two kinds. A *thing* is a countable
object — a person, a cup, a robot arm — and instances of it are separate segments. *Stuff* is an
uncountable region: sky, road, wall, floor. Every pixel belongs to exactly one segment of one kind
or the other.

For scene graphs this matters more than it first appears. A box-based dataset cannot represent
stuff as an object at all, so relations involving the road, the wall or the sky are simply absent
from Visual Genome's vocabulary. PSG has them, and its predicate distribution is therefore not
comparable with VG150's — not merely differently scaled, but drawn over a different universe.

</Step>

<Step id="s2">

## The methods, and the bottleneck

**PSGTR and PSGFormer** are the dataset's own baselines, one-stage transformers in the DETR
lineage of M7, adapted to emit segments rather than boxes.

**HiLo** observes that predicate frequency and predicate specificity pull in opposite directions,
and runs high-frequency and low-frequency branches separately before merging them. It is the
strongest reported method on the dataset and, as step 3 shows, the one the correction hit hardest.

**Pair-Net** asks a different question. Instead of improving relation classification, it measures
where the loss actually is, and finds that *pair recall* — whether the right subject-object pair
is proposed at all — is the binding constraint. A perfect relation classifier on the pairs current
methods propose would still lose most of the ground truth.

That finding is the M8 counterpart of M5's positive-rate ratio. Both say the same thing from
opposite directions: the hard part is not naming the relation, it is finding the pair.

</Step>

<Step id="s3">

## The correction, and what happened to it

Under the original PSG protocol a model could emit the same mask several times with different
predicate distributions, and every copy could score. M4 gave the arithmetic: permitting $d > 1$
masks per pair can only raise the matching, and it raises it for architectures that emit
duplicates freely — which is every one-stage method and no two-stage method.

An ECCV 2024 correction closed it. The measured effect on the three headline methods is this
module's single claim, and it is severe: roughly half the reported mean recall of the one-stage
entries, with the two-stage entries barely moving.

**The finding that matters most is not the numbers.** It is that every 2025–26 PSG paper this
project checked still reports the old figures. A correction that is published, correct and ignored
for two years is a fact about how the field works, and it is the reason this application renders
per-paper tables behind a non-comparability banner rather than merging them into a leaderboard.

The claim is marked `verified: false` because this course has not opened the correction's own
table. That is the right state for it to be in until someone does.

</Step>
